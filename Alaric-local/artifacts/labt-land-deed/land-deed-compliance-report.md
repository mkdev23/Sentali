## Executive Summary

This report provides comprehensive research on Liberian land deed compliance requirements for RWA (Real World Asset) tokenization projects targeting Liberian real estate. The Liberia Land Authority (LLA), established October 6, 2016, oversees all land administration and deed issuance in Liberia.

**Key Findings:**
- All land transactions must be registered at Liberian Land Registry for legal recognition
- Real Property Law and Land Rights Law require deed registration
- Multiple deed types exist for different ownership structures (Administrator, Warranty, Aboriginal, etc.)
- LLA provides certified copies, dispute resolution, surveys, and vetting services
- Tribal certificates can be transformed into aboriginal deeds (President-signed)

**Compliance Requirements:**
- Proof of Government Land must be evidenced by Statutory Deeds
- Legitimate chain of title required
- Government ownership must be validated through court degrees
- Customary land ownership through local/traditional authorities

**Establishment:** October 6, 2026 (Act of National Legislature)

## Land Deed Types

### Administrative Deed

**Description:** Deed issued by Administrator of an estate.
**Use Case:** Administration of deceased person's estate property
**Process:**
1. Probate Court issues Court Degree of Sale
2. Administrator/Administratrix receives authorization
3. Administrator Deed issued LLA

**Restriction:** Executer cannot sell the property during testamentary period

---

### Aboriginal Deed

**Description:** Deed for customary/indigenous land ownership
**Signed by:** President of Liberia
**Transformation Process:** Tribal certificate → President-signed deed → Transformed into private land
**Issuance Authority:** Customary land-owning communities

**Requirements:**
- Local/traditional authorities decide property disposition
- Transformation from tribal certificate to deed
- Presidential signature required
- Customary law compliance

---

### Court Decree of Sale

**Description:** Court-ordered sale of confiscated property
**Process:**
1. Court confiscates property
2. Bailiff supervises auction
3. Court Sheriff signs Sheriff Deed
4. Property transferred to buyer

**Use Case:** Government confiscation and subsequent sale

---

### Curator Deed / Sheriff Deed

**Description:** Court judgment where property confiscated and auctioned
**Signatory:** Court Sheriff
**Process:** Bailiff supervises auction
**Outcome:** Property transferred (often 50/50 distribution between parties A & B)

**Note:** These deeds often result from divorce or inheritance partition disputes

---

### Executor Deed / Testamentary Will

**Description:** Testamentary will where property is joint tenure
**Restriction:** Property cannot be sold
**Ownership Structure:** Co-ownership

**Process:**
- Property remains joint until partition
- Heirs share ownership
- Can partition if one dies or divorce

---

### Warranty Deed

**Description:** Transfer of title from one person to another
**Use:** Sale transaction between parties
**Requirements:**
- Clear title transfer
- Warranty of no claims

---

### Quit Claim Deed

**Description:** Joint ownership/partnership where parties quit claims to each other
**Distribution:** 50/50 between party A and party B
**Typical Use:**
- Inheritance partition
- Divorce property division
- Between business partners

**Restriction:** Cannot sell property during joint tenure

---

### Public Land Sale Deed

**Description:** Deed acquired from government after purchasing public land
**Issuance:** Signed by Chairman of LLA
**Requirements:**
- Government land purchase
- Tribal certificate consent from customary community
- All required steps followed

**Process:**
1. Government land purchase agreement
2. Customary community consent
3. Chairman of LLA signs deed
4. Transfer to individual/institute

---

### Lease Agreement

**Description:** Written agreement for property use for specified period
**Terms:**
- Specified term (period)
- Periodic payments (rent)
- Other terms and conditions

**Use:** Property rental without ownership transfer

---

### Aboriginal Deed Requirements

**Requirements:**
- Tribal certificate must exist first
- Local/traditional authorities approval
- Presidential signature
- Transformation into private land
- LLA registration

**Process:**
1. Community approves land for deed
2. Tribal certificate issued
3. LLA processes transformation request
4. Presidential signature
5. LLA registers aboriginal deed
6. Deed issued to community

---

### Administrator Deed Requirements

**Requirements:**
- Probate Court involvement
- Court Degree of Sale
- Administrator/Administratrix authorization
- Letter of authorization (if applicable)

**Restriction:**
- Property cannot be sold during executor tenure
- Joint tenure until partition

---

### Customary Land Ownership

**Description:** Ownership held by local/traditional authorities
**Transformation:** Through aboriginal deed process
**Authority:** Customary law decisions

---

### Tribal Certificate

**Description:** Certificate issued for customary land
**Status:** Not statutory land yet
**Transformation:** Must become aboriginal deed through presidential signature

## Land Registration Requirements

### Mandatory Registration

**Law:** Real Property Law and Land Rights Law
**Requirement:** All land transactions registered at Liberian Land Registry
**Purpose:**
- Public notice of transaction
- Legal recognition of ownership
- Chain of title validation

**Documents Required:**
- Transfer deed
- Proof of title
- Proof of ownership
- Legal requirements

**Process:**
1. Prepare transfer deed
2. Submit to LLA
3. LLA records transaction
4. Certified copy issued
5. Public notice published

---

### Land Transaction Validation

**Required for Validity:**
- Deed issued by proper authority (President, Court, Administrator, etc.)
- LLA registration completed
- Public notice made
- Chain of title verified

---

### Proof of Government Land

**Evidence Required:**
- Statutory Deeds issued
- Court Degree of Sale (if confiscated land)
- Public Land Sale Deed from Chairman of LLA
- Tribal certificate (aboriginal deed process)

**Government Land Purchase:**
1. Purchase agreement with government
2. Customary community consent
3. Chairman of LLA signs Public Land Sale Deed
4. Transfer to buyer

## Dispute Resolution

### Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR)

**Description:** Out-of-court settlement for land disputes
**LLA Role:** Mediator
**Benefits:**
- Reduces court backlog
- Accessible to those without legal representation
- Amicable resolution
- Face-to-face process

**Process:**
1. Both parties submit claims
2. LLA assigns mediator
3. Face-to-face meetings
4. Documentation of claims
5. Resolution reached
6. LLA issues ruling (if needed)

---

### Investigative Survey

**Description:** Survey to establish ownership in dispute case
**Timing:** Later part of investigative process
**Authority:** LLA conducts survey
**Use:** Establish ownership boundaries and titles

---

### Search of Deed

**Description:** Finding missing or mutilated deed
**Use Case:** Dispute settlement
**Process:**
1. Request for deed search
2. LLA searches records
3. Certified copy reissued (if found)
4. Documents corrected

---

### Vetting of Land Document

**Description:** Service to determine if land document is legal/legitimate
**Issued By:**
- Elders of customary land-owning communities (past land)
- Former Presidents of Liberia (public land)

**Process:**
1. Submit land document for vetting
2. LLA reviews document
3. Consults with elders (for customary land)
4. Requests historical records (for former government deeds)
5. Vetting result issued
